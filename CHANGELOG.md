# Changelog

All notable changes to Smart Parks Protect are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and versions follow [semantic versioning](https://semver.org/). Servers run tagged releases, never `main`.

## Unreleased

### Added

- **The Movement line reads the fixes as well as the accelerometer, and names its source** (Tim, 2026-10-28, decision D363: SP051307 drove 70 km through Namibia lying flat in the car, and its line read "still for 20 h" beside fixes 40 km apart, since the hourly accelerometer sample of a device that keeps its posture looks the same at a steady speed as parked). A valid fix more than 50 m, or more than its own accuracy, from the valid fix before it is movement whatever the accelerometer saw; the health card, the map panels and the lists show the newer of the two sources and say which: "moving, 3 h ago (positions)", "still for 20 h (accelerometer samples)". A device without an accelerometer gets the line once its fixes showed it moving. The "Device not moving" rule keeps reading the accelerometer.
- **The offline map for Protect Go is cut from several regions when a project's places lie far apart** (Tim, 2026-10-28, decision D365: the app showed the Netherlands, where the offload station stands, and nothing of Namibia, where the tracker it serves had gone). The project's features, its entities' and devices' places and the current positions of its devices and entities are grouped into regions where they lie within 100 km of one another (`MAP_EXTRACT_REGION_GAP_KM`), each padded by the margin, and one file is cut for all of them, so the Namibian coast and a Dutch village travel together without most of Africa between them. The bundle carries the regions as `areas` beside the envelope `area`, the map's state says whether its regions still cover them (`covers_areas`), and Protect Go asks for a new map when they do not.
- **A flagged fix confirms itself when the next fix agrees with it** (Tim, 2026-10-27, decision D360: SP051307 flew to Namibia and the 51 fixes of the drive from the airport were flagged as outliers against its last fix in Utrecht, 8383 km away in 42 hours, with an event each, and the drive stayed off the map). A fix that lies too far from the last valid fix is flagged as before, but when the fix after it agrees with it, the device really moved, carried by road, boat or plane: the flagged fix becomes valid at once, the position moves, and one event "Jump of 8383 km in 42.3 h confirmed by the next fix: the device moved" stands for the jump, with the figures and the fix that confirmed it. A card or a raw log settles this inside one delivery and leaves no outlier behind. A single wild fix whose successor returns to the old place stays flagged for review, as before. The two event types now have their "What this means" explanation.
- **Approve all** above the Outliers list under Curation (Tim, 2026-10-27, decision D362: 51 buttons for one drive): every flagged fix still waiting becomes valid in one go through the same audited, reversible correction the row's button writes, under the project's approval rule; `POST /projects/{id}/curation/outliers/approve-all` takes an optional device.

### Fixed

- **A card's files decoded out of time order no longer lose or invent movement** (Tim, 2026-10-28, decision D364: SP051307's offload decoded the evening's file before the day's, so the eleven samples of the drive from Windhoek got no activity at all, and the evening's first sample was judged against one twelve hours older, a change of 13.9 while the car stood, which set the last movement). The decoder now judges every accelerometer sample against the sample before it in time, read from the table, derives the activity of the sample after a late frame anew, writes a derived value a re-decode corrects, and recomputes the last movement when the sample that set it no longer qualifies.
- **An entity its device left no longer shows that device's fresh status on the live map** (Tim, 2026-10-27, decision D361: entity Tim in Smart Parks read "last status 1 h ago, moving 8 h ago, uptime 4 d" from a device that had moved to another project and another country two days before). The map's features take the device from the assignment valid now, as the Entities page does; an entity nothing tracks today keeps its last position and last seen and shows no device health, battery, movement or status. Its page says "no device tracks it today" where "Data received" read "never".

- **An assignment boundary moves after the fact, with its neighbour** (Tim, 2026-10-26, decisions D355 to D358, ADR 0041: SP051307 was moved from Smart Parks to Smart Parks - LP0 and from entity Tim to entity SP051307 "from now", while it really changed hands a day earlier, one second after its last KPN uplink, and nothing could move that boundary). The device page's new Assignments tab lists the project assignments and the entity assignments, the current row first, each with its dates and, when a boundary was moved, who changed it and why, and the two cards carry Assign, Move and Release; "Edit" on a row opens a dialog with From and To in the project's timezone, "Use the last uplink" from the device's own messages, an optional note, and one line saying what else moves; where the project and the entity changed at the same moment, the dialog offers, ticked, to move the other assignment along. (Built first with a two-lane timeline, a Transfers card, a pencil per edge and a required reason; Tim, the same day: too complicated for most users, so the lists, one Edit and a note.) The neighbouring row's edge moves with the boundary, so the timeline keeps no gap and no overlap; a moment in the future, one that would leave a row empty or cross another, or an entity row outside the device's project is refused in words. The records between the old and the new moment change hands through the attribution job; raw messages never change; every changed row gets an audit row with the reason. API: `GET /devices/{id}/assignments`, `GET /devices/{id}/last-message`, `PATCH .../project-assignments/{id}/boundary`, `PATCH .../entity-assignments/{id}/boundary` and `POST /devices/{id}/assignments/correct`, each with a preview. MCP: `get_device_assignments` and `update_assignment_boundary` under the new scope `assignments:write`, held for the person's confirmation by default with a summary naming every row, the instants and the window. No migration.
- **A station's clock behind the records no longer dates the offload** (Tim, 2026-10-24, decision D354: SP052104's card, read into Protect that morning through Protect Go, showed "Last offload 2 days ago" for the station and the tracker, since the station stamps its lines two days early). A frame cannot be received before the tracker made the records it carries, so the decoder notes the newest record of every station frame and how far the station's stamp runs behind it, and the Offload page, the station card and the live map's station panel read the moment of an offload as the later of the two, marked "(clock behind)" with how far the clock is behind at least. A card decoded before this version keeps the station's time until "Decode again" on its file.
- **A device's Traffic tab, with the depth of the Traffic page** (Tim, 2026-10-21, decision D352: the traffic table is where an investigation of a device starts, and in portrait it was useless). The device page's Network tab is the Traffic tab now, the table first: the window presets up to two years, a type filter, "Problems only" (failed and unassigned), pages of fifty with Load more and the count of the window, refreshed every fifteen seconds; the identities and the control card follow. Every column stays on a phone, the time in view while the rest scrolls sideways. The entity page's tab follows, old links to `?tab=network` still land, and the traffic API gained `status`, keyset paging (`before`, `before_id`) and `/traffic/count`.
- **Dead letters are resolved in one click, and a garbage frame no longer makes one** (Tim, 2026-10-21, decision D349: two frames that did not decode raised a system alert that took five clicks to clear). A decoder refusal no retry can change is a failed source event on Needs attention with "Decode again" and nothing more; the bus keeps dead letters for retries exhausted, a crashed handler and an unsupported message version. Those are resolved from the alert itself ("Resolve the dead letters" in the alert's dialog, with a link to look at them first), or on the Dead letters tab with "Resolve all" and "Retry all" per topic beside the per-row actions; the tab and its topic are in the page's address, so the alert opens the right list. The alert's explanation names Needs attention instead of System health.
- **An offload station's panel on the live map is about the station** (Tim, 2026-10-21, decision D350: it read like a tracker's, "last seen never" with a battery and nothing of the station). The panel now shows the station's last report of itself, the SD card (usable with its free share, absent or faulted, unknown), whether it listens and the writes pending, the visits, the trackers served in the last 30 days with the last offload, and the cards uploaded; "Last seen" is its last sign of life, the uptime the report's, and a static station has no track, heatmap or follow button. The station card's API carries `card_ok`, the decoder's one reading of a usable card.
- **The Offload page names the trackers of other projects its stations served, and offers the move** (Tim, 2026-10-21, decision D348: a new station's card was read and nothing seemed to happen; the tracker was a device of another project, so its records went to that project's entity and map). A device belongs to one project at a time, so the page now has a section "Trackers of other projects": the device with a link into its project, the project, its entity, its address, the stations that heard it and its last offload, and "Move to this project" for a server admin or a project admin of both projects, which opens the move dialog with this project preset. The field bundle carries the same trackers (address and names, never a key), so Protect Go can name a card's frames of them rather than offer to add a device Protect already has.
- **Firmware 8 devices are read and set in their own settings format** (Tim, 2026-10-21, decision D346: a RangerEdge on firmware 8.1 connected over Bluetooth in the station dialog but gave no EUI, name or keys). Firmware 7.4 and later address every setting by a family byte and an id inside it, and send a settings record as `[family][id][len][value]`; Protect read the firmware 7 layout and looked the result up in the 7.3.0 catalogue. The OpenCollar driver now ships a second catalogue, `catalog-v8.json` (firmware 8.1.0, built from the firmware's own settings table by `scripts/opencollar_catalog_v8.py`, with the LP0 cluster settings and commands of 8.1), and the firmware a device reports picks the generation everywhere: the decoder's reading of port 3 and port 30 frames, the settings known per device and the Settings tab, the expected intervals, the `SET_SETTING`, `REQUEST_SETTING` and `SET_GNSS_INTERVAL` commands (which write the family byte and look the setting up in that firmware's table), and the browser, which reads the status first and asks for the catalogue of the version it names (`?firmware=` on the two catalogue routes). An unknown firmware is read as the newest, as the port layouts are.
- **The offload station's report in its versions 2 and 3** (the firmware branch of 2026-10-20, decision D345). The OpenCollar driver reads the port 23 report of a station in every version the firmware has published: version 2 brought the card's size in whole MB and its free share in whole percent (unknown together until the reader's units are verified), version 3 the observations `sd_card_missing` and `sdfs_error`, a completed card check that found none and a storage fault latched until the station's own test passes. The measurements `lp0_sd_capacity_mb` and `lp0_sd_free_percent` (migration 0056), an **SD card free** health line (warning under 10 %), and "SD card usable" is no when the station reports a missing card or a fault, even while its card bits are unobserved: `lp0_sd_card_ok` in the driver module is the one reading, the decoder's measurement and the Offload page's `card_ok` (whose Setup column says "card not usable" now). The station card shows the card's space and the two observations, the Offload page the free share beside the card's state. A report version Protect does not know is refused by name. The vendored firmware decoder is the branch's at 7bae042 and the statistics test runs it over reports of every version.
- **The field bundle carries a revision, and a cheap read of it** (decision D344, for Protect Go to follow the project by itself: Tim, 2026-10-19, the app should notice a changed project, new devices, stations, trackers or a map, without a hand on its Server page). `revision` on the bundle is a fingerprint of its content, the same until the project changes; `GET /projects/{id}/field-bundle/revision` answers it alone, unaudited.
- **A card from a station the project does not have is offered as a new station** (Tim, 2026-10-19, decision D343: a clear process for an SD card file of an unregistered station). The upload keeps nothing of such files and answers with the station's EUI (`unknown_stations` on the upload's answer, no refusal); the page opens a dialog where the station gets its label name, is made with the EUI as serial and set up as an offload station in one go, and the same files go up again to it. Its place follows on its page.
- **A tracker added after its cards were read gets its files processed** (Tim, 2026-10-19, decision D342). The decoder's walk over the frames that waited for a device already decodes them; now it also sums the SD card files it touched anew, so the Files section shows the records, the period and the figures per tracker without a second decode (`refresh_summary`).
- **The Offload page counts a station's frames by their arrival, and names the unknown devices it heard** (Tim, 2026-10-19: SP052099 said "46 frames of 1 trackers" for 13,671 receptions, because its clock stamps a month behind and the 30-day window went by that clock). The window is the arrival at Protect now, the upload of a card; the figures read "frames of N trackers and M unknown".
- **The files filter tells stations from trackers** (Tim, 2026-10-19): the device list of the Offload page's Files section is grouped, Stations and Trackers.
- **An unknown device is added as a station or as a tracker, and the page says which it looks like** (Tim, 2026-10-19, decision D341: with the new firmware a station also records the status reports of other stations in range). The ports a frame carries in the clear tell them apart: a station's own status report travels on port 23, a tracker's stored stream on port 29. The Unknown devices table has a "Looks like" column, and the add dialog, now "Add the device", opens on the choice "A tracker" or "An offload station", preset from the ports; a station is set up at once, with its entity and its gateway row, its place to follow on its page. `ports` and `kind_hint` on the overview's unknown rows.
- **"Unknown devices"** is the name of the Offload page's section of addresses no device has (Tim, 2026-10-19), and its add action is a plus in the first column, so a phone reaches it without scrolling.
- **The Offload page's unknown devices say which stations heard them** ("Heard by", each a link to the station; Tim, 2026-10-19), and a tracker without an entity and without a position has no live map pin, since the map would show nothing for it.
- **A device's EUI that contradicts the cards is flagged, and corrected on the device page** (Tim, 2026-10-19: Luka's station SP052099 carried a typed serial while its card files named it 0016C001F0969D7F, and its LP0 address was a tracker's). The Offload page's Setup column says "serial differs from the cards" for a station whose files carry another EUI, and "the card names another tracker" for a tracker whose address a v5 card attributes to another EUI (`files_eui` on the overview's stations, `card_dev_eui` and `serial_number` on its trackers). "Edit" on the device page changes name and serial together; a serial another device carries is refused by name. Under LP0 offload, "Decode them now" hands the waiting frames to the decoder after a correction (`POST /devices/{id}/lp0-credentials/walk`).
- **A device or an entity made by mistake is deleted from its page** (Tim, 2026-10-19: a tracker added twice under two names had no way out). "Delete" on the device page, for whoever manages the device, removes a device without data: no message received, no log file, no record; a device with data is refused with its counts, and is retired instead or, by a server admin who typed DELETE, deleted with every message, record and file (`DELETE /devices/{id}`, `with_data=true`; the time series go segment by segment and week by week, one transaction each, as TimescaleDB's compressed chunks require; the log files' objects leave the bucket). Its identities stay on their networks, unlinked, and linking such an identity to the right device from its page takes the existing row instead of refusing it. "Delete" on the entity page, for `entities:write`, removes an entity with its assignments; one with history is archived under Edit, and the server refuses one that records point at.
- **A taken device name is refused in words** (Tim, 2026-10-19: renaming a tracker to the name of the Iridium desk device answered with the database's own line). Device names are unique across the server; the rename and the creation now say which device carries the name and in what project, and what to do when it is the same hardware. The constraints a person runs into (device name and serial, entity, feature and group names in a project) answer in words everywhere else too; the rename dialog keeps the refusal in view.
- **A device is renamed on its page** (Tim, 2026-10-19: a device added with a slip in its name had no way to be renamed; an entity had, under Edit). "Rename" in the device page's header, for whoever manages the device (decision D337), changes the name alone; the serial number stays.
- **A tracker a card names before Protect knows it is added from the Offload page** (Tim, 2026-10-19, decision D338: the trackers may not be known yet when the stations' cards arrive). Every row of "Unknown devices" (called "Addresses no device has" until Tim renamed it the same day) starts with a plus that adds the tracker: a new device, the station dialog's form with the LP0 address, the DevEUI the card named and a name filled in, read over Bluetooth or typed, the keys optional; or a device of the project that takes the address and, when known, the keys. The address alone claims the frames a card holds for it (`lp0` without keys on `POST /projects/{id}/devices`, `dev_addr` alone on `PUT /devices/{id}/lp0-credentials`): they become the device's, fail plainly for want of keys, the Setup column says "keys not set", and the keys, set later on the device page, hand them to the decoder at once. `?add=<address>` on the Offload page opens the dialog, the way Protect Go hands a tracker over.
- **A device found again on the network keeps nothing waiting** (decision D339). A DevEUI identity links itself to the device whose serial it is, wherever the message comes from, so a tracker set up from a card is recognised on the LoRaWAN server without a hand. When the network names the device otherwise than Protect does, Needs attention lists it under Names with "Rename to …" and "Keep Protect's name" (`GET /attention/names`, `POST /attention/names/{identity_id}/rename` and `/keep`); the DevEUI stays the serial either way and is never guessed. The add device form asks for the label name (SP…) while the DevEUI stands in.
- **A project admin manages the devices of the project** (Tim, 2026-10-19, decision D337: Luka, a project admin of LP0, had to be made server admin to add stations). The Devices page has "Add device" for `devices:write`, the form of the station dialog with every device type, Bluetooth read for an OpenCollar type; on a device's page a project admin changes the name, type, status and location source, sets the picture and links an identity (a DevEUI on a network), choosing the data source by name (`GET /projects/{id}/data-sources`, names and adapters alone). `PATCH /devices/{id}`, the picture and `POST /devices/{id}/identities` take `devices:write` in the device's current project; a device in no project, the import, the device types and the data sources stay with the server admin.
- **An offload station is static or mobile** (Tim, 2026-10-18, decision D336). Static, the default, stands where a person placed it (decision D261). Mobile travels, on a vehicle or with a ranger, and reports its position itself like a tracker: the station dialog offers the choice, the station card on the device page shows it and switches it ("Make it mobile", "Make it static", `devices:write`), the Offload page marks a mobile station with a badge, its Setup column asks for no place of it ("no position yet" until a fix arrives), and the stations map draws a mobile station at its last known position with the time of that fix. Making a station mobile takes a place set by hand away. `OffloadStationSetup.mobile` on `POST /projects/{id}/offload/stations`; `mobile`, `location` and `location_at` on the overview's stations; `mobile` on the station card's read, which now counts a station set up before its first card.

## v2.9.0, 2026-09-19

Phase 29 closed: the movement methods that the v2.8.0 tag left unbuilt, the autocorrelation-corrected home range and the movement strategy, read by Tim on the dev server over SP050969 (corrected 95% range 38.9 ha against the plain KDE's 25.6, resident with a clear margin) and released the same day. With them the review of the two days' contact tracing work and the satellite test flake, found to be a test's own doing. No migration; the schema stays at revision 0046.

### Added

- The movement analysis has a fourth home range method, **KDE corrected for autocorrelation** (phase 29, decision D244). Hourly fixes of an animal whose position takes two days to decorrelate are not seven hundred independent looks at its range but a few dozen, and a plain KDE drawn from them is too small. The method draws a variogram of the fixes, fits an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck curve to it, takes the effective number of independent fixes from the autocorrelation time it finds, and widens the kernel accordingly; on simulated tracks with a known range it is right on average where the plain KDE runs thirty percent short. It is our own approximation and is named as such, never AKDE. When the period does not show the whole range, the fixes are still spreading at the longest lags drawn, it says so instead of drawing one. The autocorrelation time and the effective fixes stand beside the area so a reader sees why it is larger.

- The movement analysis reads a **movement strategy** off the net squared displacement (phase 29): resident, migratory, dispersal or nomadic, from the four curves of Bunnefeld and others (2011) fitted to the NSD per day, the lowest corrected Akaike information winning. The class is on the subject's card and in the table with the figures a reader wants beside it, the departure and return days and the distance between ranges for a migrant, the departure and distance for a disperser, and the fitted curve is drawn dotted over the NSD chart. A margin under two AICc units reads "unclear" rather than a class the data does not support, and a period under sixty days gets no class and says why. The switch is on by default and in the method options.

### Fixed

- Clearing a device's fixed place left it standing there. The place is stamped on the current states and no record put it there, so nothing but a rebuild takes it off: the device and the entity it is on stayed at the old place, marked "fixed place", after the place was cleared. Clearing now rebuilds both from what the device reports, which for a reader that reports nothing is nowhere. (Review of the two days' work, 2026-09-19.)

- Clearing or correcting a device's Bluetooth address left the contacts the old address had resolved pointing at the device. An address typed from a label can be wrong, and the correction has to undo what the wrong one did: the sightings that named the old address are read again, and go back to unknown neighbours or to the one device that does carry those octets.

- The **Human presence** event and its description came in English on a Dutch interface; both are in the catalogue now (decision D240).

- The contact tracing charts counted in UTC: the rose of the hour of day and the contacts per day were two hours off for a Dutch project. Both count on the project's own clock now, as the movement and grazing modules do.

- A contact tracing run with a comparison period showed every sampling warning twice, once per period. Each is shown once.

- ADR 0036 still called the clock rule's reach over positions an open question; it was decided on 2026-09-19 (D262: positions keep the time their device claimed) and the ADR says so. The contact tracing guide now describes where a sighting-only pair is put on the map, which was built and not written down.

- Devices bolted to posts read "moving". A still device's accelerometer does not read the same twice: the OpenCollar's is 8-bit over ±100 m/s² in two-count steps, so as the temperature drifts it reports changes of 0.784 m/s² per axis, 1.36 when all three drift at once, and the rule called anything above 1.0 movement. Measured on the 45 PWN scanners over eleven days: 540 false crossings, and 33 of the 45 shown as moving. Movement now needs one change of 2.0 m/s², or two of 1.4 running, which leaves about 25 of those 540 while a single clear jump, somebody picking the device up, still counts at once. (Tim, 2026-09-19.)

Rather than tag a half-finished phase under its own number, its delivered half rides here and the number is skipped, as phases 22 and 28 were folded into v2.6.0.

What the phase learned, which is the part worth keeping: nine defects surfaced in the last day and only three were caught by the test suite. ### Added

- [Contact tracing](docs/analytics/contact-tracing.md), the guide: what a contact is and is not, the form, the three warnings and why each exists, and what comes out. It says plainly, twice, that the module infers no transmission and that a signal is never converted to metres.

- The contact network is drawn, in the interface and in the PDF (phase 31): subjects as nodes sized by how many contacts they had, pairs as edges thickened by how often they met and dashed when only one kind of evidence saw them. A subject that met nobody stays on the picture as a small unattached dot, leaving it out would answer "who met" instead of "who met whom", and the animals that met nothing are often the finding. On screen the layout is a force simulation that can be dragged and roamed; on paper it is a circle in the subjects' own order, because a document somebody prints, files and compares against last month's cannot move its nodes between runs. The result map gains **Where pairs met**: a point between the two animals, sized by how often, which is the answer a map can give that a table cannot.

- **Contact tracing**, a fourth analysis module (phase 31, decision D255): who met whom, from two kinds of evidence at once, the sightings a device reported over Bluetooth, and the proximity two subjects' fixes show within a configurable distance and time. A pair both kinds found is solid; a pair only the fixes found rests on how often the animals report, and the run says so rather than leaving a reader to guess. It produces a contact network, a pair table worst first, a row per subject of how many others it met, contacts per day and by hour of day, and a point on the map where each pair met, usually a water hole. `max_distance_m` (100 m) and `max_time_s` (10 min) are the two limits Tim asked to be configurable; `bluetooth` and `proximity` each switch off. It infers no transmission and says so in its own limitations, and a signal is banded rather than converted to metres, which would need a calibration nobody has. `ANALYSIS_MODULES` gains `contact_tracing`.

- `scripts/refile_scan_records.py` reports, and on `--apply` removes, the scan records an earlier version of the clock rule filed at a device's claimed time instead of at its delivery, so that a reprocess writes them back where they belong. Moving them in place is not possible: `time` partitions the hypertable and a row cannot be updated across chunks. Every row it touches is derived from a source event that is kept for ever.

- A scan says how many devices it detected, and that number is now reported beside the sightings that arrived (Tim, 2026-09-18). Only what fits in one message goes over the air and the rest stays in the device's flash, so counting the sightings understates what was there: a collar in a herd detects more than it can send. The Bluetooth contacts card names both, the scans, what they detected, and what reached Protect, and says so plainly when they differ. The `ble_contacts` metric was already the device's own count, so the chart has always been the honest one.

- How much a scanner hears, as a line (Tim, 2026-09-18). Every Bluetooth scan leaves one `ble_contacts` measurement, the devices it saw, zero included, so the Contacts row of the live map panel unfolds a chart the way the battery and the uptime do, over a day, a week or a month, and the same figure is a metric anywhere else: the data explorer, a dashboard, a rule. A scan that saw nothing is a zero and not a gap, because the gaps mean something different: the device was not looking. Migration 0045 registers it, and `human_presence` with it.

- A device placed by a sighting says who heard it: a **Heard by** row on the live map panel of the tag and of the animal it is on, linking to that scanner on the same map (Tim, 2026-09-18). A proximity position is somebody else's word for where a device was, and the somebody is the point, a rabbit is at a place because a named reader heard it, and a person reading that wants to go to the reader.

- `scripts/place_devices_from_tags.py` reads the coordinates some networks keep in their own device tags and sets static places from them, deliberately a script somebody runs and reads, never a mechanism (Tim, 2026-09-18). A tag is somebody else's metadata: nobody promises it is current and nothing says who last edited it, so a place taken from one silently would be a position on the map with no accountable origin. A device that already has a place is left alone, because a measurement made in the field outranks a tag.

- `scripts/reprocess_source_events.py` decodes stored messages again, for data that arrived before its decoder existed. A source event is decoded once and nothing revisits it, so the 2,112 Bluetooth scans the PWN readers had sent since 8 September 2026 sat decoded into nothing. Decoding is idempotent, so a second run only counts duplicates and a half-finished run is resumed by running it again. It takes `--port`, `--device` and `--since`, and refuses to reprocess everything.

- A **Bluetooth scanner** entity type, under Equipment, with EarthRanger's radar icon (Tim, 2026-09-18). The catalogue had "Stationary radio" and "Sensor" and neither says what a reader on a post does: it listens for the tags on animals, or for the phones people carry, and its place is set by hand because it does not move. Migration 0044 adds it to servers that already hold the catalogue.

- Bluetooth contacts: what a device heard around it is data (phase 30, decisions D252 to D261, ADR 0036). An OpenCollar with scanning on reports the last three octets of every Bluetooth address near it and how strong the signal was; those sightings are now decoded, attributed and stored as a record type of their own beside positions, measurements, states and events, and shown on the device's Data tab with the scan settings above them, an empty list means "it met nobody" only if the device was looking. Three octets are not an identity, so each sighting is resolved once and honestly: to the one device in the project it can be, to an unknown neighbour kept and named by its octets, or to nothing at all when more than one device could be it. Recording a device's own address repairs its past in both directions. Supported in the three ways the feature is used: collars seeing other collars, a reader on a post seeing tags on animals that carry no GNSS, and a collar or reader seeing the phones people carry. [Bluetooth contacts](docs/devices/bluetooth-contacts.md).

### Changed

- The areas by pressure are warm too, so green on a result map means vegetation and nothing else (Tim, 2026-09-18). That ramp ran green to coral, which was not only a second green: coral is lighter than the dark green below it, so "twice the herd average" came out paler than "above average" and the ramp reversed at its most important step. - The legends under a result map are built alike: the same label column, the same swatch width and the value each colour starts at under it, so two of them stacked line up. The use intensity says the animal-hours each colour begins at instead of only "less use" and "more", which left the reader guessing whether more meant an hour or a week.

- The use intensity layer of a grazing result map is warm instead of green (Tim, 2026-09-18). Measured against the vegetation ramp, warm separates best of the candidates under both red-green colour blindnesses, and unlike a blue ramp it is never mistaken for the water the base maps draw. It also says the right thing: warm is how hard the ground was used, green is what it grows. The green cells had been hard to see even on their own, sitting on the green wash of the areas.

- A result map dropped any geometry kind it had not been told about. It took a fixed list of kinds and kept the ones the run had, so contact tracing's "Where pairs met" points were filtered out of the layer and offered no chip, silently. The run now says which kinds it has and the fixed list only decides the drawing order, so a module that emits something new draws it instead of vanishing.

- A contact found only by Bluetooth had no place on the map. The design gives a contact the midpoint of a proximity **or the place of the device that did the hearing**, and only the first half was built, so for a study of fixed readers, where every contact is a sighting and the reader's place is known exactly, the map was empty while the answer sat in a column. A sighting now takes the observer's place: its post when it stands on one, its nearest fix when it was walking about. The device that was heard has no say, since it is the one whose position the sighting was meant to establish.

- A position one device gave another was counted as that device's own fix. `device_fix()`, the one place that separates a fix from an estimate, and which every track, analysis, rule and export reads, tested for the network's estimates alone. Phase 30 added two more kinds without teaching it about them, so a Bluetooth tag looked as though it had fixed itself at the reader that heard it, two tags at one reader came out nought metres apart, and the movement of an animal carrying no GNSS at all was computed from where other hardware stood. - One scan was filed under two different times. The clock rule (decision D259) reached a scan's sightings but not the scan itself, so a reader whose clock runs 45 hours behind had its contacts at the delivery and the scan's own record and reading two days earlier, and the new chart of what it heard therefore ended before its own sightings did. A driver now marks the records whose time is the same clock reading its contacts carry, and those are corrected with them. Only those: a genuine backfill over a live channel carries the past on purpose, and positions are still left alone, since rewriting when an animal was somewhere moves tracks and attribution.

- A stored log carries many scans into one delivery, and each scan claimed to have reported its own sightings plus every sighting before it in the stream. The count is per scan now. Only a flash log or a Bluetooth download reaches this; a scan over the air is one message and was always right.

- A rebuild of the current state took a placed device off the map. The rebuild reads the positions a device produced and a device placed by hand produces none, so it blanked the row, and assigning such a device to an entity queues an attribution job, which meant a scanner vanished from the map a moment after it was put there. A place is not a record and no rebuild can find it, so it is stamped back on afterwards, for the device and for the entity it is on. (2026-09-18.)

- An animal wearing only a Bluetooth tag said "Last seen: never" on the live map while a reader had heard it minutes before. Being heard moved the tag's last seen but not the animal's, and for an animal with no tracker of its own there is nothing else that ever could.

In the field a reader is put up, it scans for a week, and only then does somebody measure where it stands and type the tags' addresses; every sighting from before that afternoon said when but never where, and for a rabbit wearing only a tag that is the whole of what could ever have been known about it. Both repairs are safe to run again and write nothing twice. (217 of the PWN sightings were in that state, 2026-09-18.)
