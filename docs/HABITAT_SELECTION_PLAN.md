## 1. What hrHSA is

hrHSA ("High-resolution Habitat Selection Analysis", https://hrhsa.readthedocs.io, https://github.com/PantheraPaulus/hrHSA) is a Python framework that fits habitat selection models from telemetry and environmental rasters. Read on 2026-10-08: the documentation (index, getting started, theory, implementation, performance, case studies, acknowledgements), the repository's `pyproject.toml`, `LICENSE`, `src/hsa/__init__.py`, `src/hsa/types.py`, `src/hsa/rsf/{__init__,base,frequentist,cv,validation,surface}.py`, `src/hsa/movement/geometry.py`, `src/hsa/compute/{raster,io}.py` and the commit list.

- Version 0.1.0, BSD 3-Clause, copyright 2026 Paul Kasko and Ralph Kühn; 34 commits between 2026-09-13 and 2026-10-08, one committer, no release tag, not on PyPI; installed with `pip install -e ".[hpc]"` from a clone; imported as `hsa`.
- Models: resource selection functions (RSF: logistic regression of used against sampled available points, relative selection `w(x) = exp(x'β)`), step selection functions (SSF: a choice set per step with movement-informed alternatives, conditional logit) and integrated SSF (iSSF: the movement kernel, `L`, `log L`, `cos θ`, fitted with the selection). Frequentist inference through statsmodels (Newton, L-BFGS) or Bayesian through PyMC with hierarchical individual coefficients `β_i = μ + b_i` and partial pooling.
- Validation: leave-one-individual-out (LOIO) with a continuous Boyce index and calibration curves, temporal blocking, posterior predictive checks. Prediction: a relative selection surface as an xarray raster.
- Inputs: relocations as a GeoDataFrame with an individual id, a timestamp and a point in a projected CRS (`prepare_trajectory_data` projects, rounds and drops duplicate fixes); covariates as an xarray DataArray with dims `band`, `y`, `x` (a `time` dimension for dynamic fields), band names as the predictor names, the same CRS as the relocations. The getting started opens a Zarr stack; the sampler (`sample_raster_stack_chunked`) takes any DataArray or Dataset of that shape, so an in-memory stack built from GeoTIFFs with rioxarray is the same thing.
- The RSF path, as the code reads: `FrequentistRSF(reloc, env, spec=FeatureSpec(linear=[...], quadratic=[...], interactions=[...], categorical=[...]), id_col=, timestamp_col=, domain=None, domain_quantile=0.95)`; one availability domain per individual, the minimum convex polygon at the quantile, unless a common domain is given; `.fit(sampling_factor=10, thin_dt="12h", seed=42, method="newton")` returns a `FrequentistRSFFit` whose `coefficients()` is a table of term, estimate, standard error, p value, lower and upper, and whose `predict_surface(env)` is a DataArray with one band `rsf`; `.validate(LeaveOneIndividualOut(...))` runs `leave_one_individual_out_rsf`, which trains on every other animal and answers a summary per fold, the coefficients per fold, the Boyce bins, the calibration bins and the fold diagnostics.
- Dependencies: `numpy<2.5`, pandas, geopandas, shapely, pyproj, scipy, scikit-learn, statsmodels, xarray, rioxarray, rasterio, matplotlib; optional PyMC and ArviZ (Bayesian), Dask and dask-jobqueue (HPC), Zarr, Earth Engine. Python 3.10 or later.
- The case studies: 26 ground pangolins (July 2024 to April 2026, NDVI, Sentinel-1 VH, radar edge metrics, slope, soil; a hierarchical RSF), 21 lions (river proximity by season) and 5 bearded vultures (an iSSF with terrain and ERA5 wind and heat). The pangolin study looks like Smart Parks data; whether the authors ran it on exports of ours is a question for Tim.

## 2. Why a module, and why these limits

The admission rule of the analytics brief (`docs/ANALYTICS_PHASE1_PLAN.md`, section 20) asks that a module be used repeatedly, read data Protect already holds, support a real decision, be easier than the external tool and cost maintenance in proportion. Habitat selection meets the first three for the ecologists Protect serves: where an animal chooses to be, against what was available, is the question behind every zoning and corridor decision, and the fixes are here. The fourth is the honest doubt: the GeoParquet export of decision D312 already feeds hrHSA directly, and an ecologist with Python has the whole framework there. The module earns its place by what the export cannot do: the covariates fetched, cached and projected for the project's area without a GIS session, the run kept with its parameters and its warnings beside the movement and grazing runs, the surface on the same map, a report, and a person without Python getting a validated answer. The fifth sets the shape: the first step is the frequentist RSF with LOIO alone (D313), on a worker image of its own (D314), with the Bayesian fits and the step selection models as a second step once the first has run on a real project.

Three cautions the authors state are the module's own limitations, printed on every run: use is not habitat quality (an ideal free distribution puts animals where they are, not where it is best for them); a coefficient says how the animal redistributed relative to what was available, so the availability domain decides every contrast; and the selection surface is relative, never a probability of occurrence.

## 4. The data flow of a run

1. **Subjects and fixes.** Entities by id, group or type, at most `MAX_SUBJECTS_HABITAT` (40: LOIO fits one model per animal); the period at most `MAX_DAYS`; the fixes through `load_trajectory` (device fixes, valid, effective time and geometry, attributed by the assignment history), `exclude_impossible` and the stop rule of D303 off by default (a parked receiver's drift is used habitat too, and the fold would thin it; the switch is on the form).
2. **The area.** The union of the animals' domains (hrHSA's MCPs at the quantile, computed by us with the same rule so the rasters cover them) buffered by `area_buffer_m` (1,000 m), in the UTM zone of its centroid (`utm_srid` in `primitives/spatial.py`). The grid's resolution starts at the finest source (30 m for the DEM, 10 m for NDVI, taken as 30 m) and doubles until the area fits `MAX_RASTER_CELLS` (4 million: a 60 by 60 km park at 30 m), so a large park answers coarser rather than refusing (architecture 13.10).
3. **The covariates** (D315), each a GeoTIFF in the cache or fetched now, reprojected onto the run's grid with rioxarray (`reproject_match`, bilinear for continuous layers), stacked as one DataArray with the band names the form chose:
    - `ndvi`: the mean over the period, Sentinel-2 L2A cloud masked, one openEO job per run area and period (`save_result` as GTiff; the backend lists GTiff with deflate compression). Cached by area hash and period.
    - `elevation` and `slope`: `COPERNICUS_30` over the area, one openEO job per area, cached without a period; the slope in degrees from the elevation with numpy on the run's grid.
    - `distance_to_<type>`: for a feature type of the project, the distance from each cell centre to the nearest feature of that type, `ST_Distance` on the geography in one statement over the grid's cell centres (bounded by the cell count), cached by area hash and the features' newest `updated_at`.
    - a project layer: a GeoTIFF a project admin uploaded with a name, kept in the `analysis-layers` bucket with its CRS, extent and resolution read on upload; continuous or categorical as marked.
4. **The fit.** `prepare_trajectory_data` with `source_crs` 4326 and the UTM target, `round_freq` none, duplicates dropped; `FrequentistRSF(..., spec=FeatureSpec(linear=chosen, quadratic=chosen_quadratic), id_col="entity_id", timestamp_col="time", domain_quantile=q).fit(sampling_factor=f, thin_dt=t, seed=42)`; progress per step (fixes, area, each layer, the fit, each LOIO fold, the surface).
5. **Validation.** `rsf.validate(LeaveOneIndividualOut(heldout="all", sampling_factor_train=f, n_background=100_000, seed=42))` when the run has at least `MIN_SUBJECTS_LOIO` (3) animals; the summary and the Boyce bins per fold go into the document; a fold that fails is a warning, not a failed run.
6. **The surface.** `fit.predict_surface(env)` on the run's grid, then read onto the analysis grid's cells (the mean of the surface pixels in each cell), at most `MAX_SURFACE_CELLS` (2,000), the cell size doubled until it fits, as `vegetation_grid` does; the cells are `Geometry` rows of kind `selection` with the value and its rank; the domains are kind `domain` polygons per animal.
7. **The document.** Summary per animal and for the fleet (fixes, thinned fixes, available points, the Boyce index), the tables `coefficients`, `validation` (per held-out animal), `layers` (name, source, resolution, period, cells with a value), the charts `estimates` (bar with the interval, drawn as a line series of two points per term until `Chart` learns an error bar), `boyce` (one line per animal), the warnings and the provenance with hrHSA's commit in `sources`.

Bounds: 40 animals, 366 days, 200,000 fixes per animal before thinning, 4 million raster cells, 2,000 surface cells, 5,000 geometries, a 4 MiB document; the worker's 900 s timeout holds for 40 LOIO folds of a logistic regression on a few hundred thousand rows each (statsmodels Newton converges in under a second on such a fit; measured on the first real run and recorded in `docs/operations/benchmarks.md`). Memory: one stack of at most four million cells by at most eight bands of float32 is 128 MB.

## 8. Exit criteria

- On the dev server, a run over at least six animals of one project with NDVI, slope and the distance to the project's sites answers a coefficient table with intervals, a Boyce index per held-out animal, and a selection surface on the map that reads against the fixes; the PDF carries the same.
- The same fixes and the same three layers given to hrHSA outside Protect give the same coefficients to four decimals.
- A run with the `analysis` service on the lean image fails at once with `MODULE_UNAVAILABLE` and a plain message; the API, the decoder and every other service build and run unchanged, and `tests/shared/test_analysis_boundary.py` holds.
- A project without the openEO account can still run on an uploaded GeoTIFF and a distance layer; a project with neither is told so on the form before a run is queued.
- The access matrix holds for the new routes; a member scoped to some animals runs on those alone.

## 11. As built, 2026-10-08

H1 to H7 landed on 2026-10-08 in one session, in the order of section 7. Where the build departs from the sections above:

- **The distance layers are not PostGIS.** `primitives/habitat.py` computes `distance_to_<type>` on the run's grid with a shapely `STRtree` over the project's features projected to the grid's CRS: faster than a round trip per cell, bounded by the grid, and the worker already holds the features. The API's `project_layers` still decides which types a project may name from the features it has.
- **hrHSA needs its `dask` extra.** `hsa.rsf` imports dask at module level although the authors list it as optional; the image's lock carries `hrhsa[dask]`. The lock holds 109 packages (numpy 2.4.2, pandas 3.0.6, geopandas 1.2.0, rasterio 1.5.2, statsmodels 0.15.0, xarray 2026.9.0, scipy 1.18.1, pyproj 3.8.0). The decoder is in the image's dev group because the API test helpers import it.
- **One `uv sync` in the Dockerfile.** The workspace packages are path dependencies of the image project, not workspace members, so the dependency layer cannot be built before the sources are copied; the sync runs once after the copy, and the uv cache keeps a code change to seconds. `INSTALL_DEV=1` adds the test group for CI.
- **The lean API reads a GeoTIFF header itself.** `shared/analysis/geotiff.py` parses TIFF 6.0 and BigTIFF with the GeoTIFF 1.1 keys, so an upload without a CRS, with more than one band or outside the size bound is refused by the API without rasterio.
- **Ansible needs nothing.** The server builds through `docker compose build`, which builds the analysis image beside the others.
- **A `Fit` carries the rank breaks**, so the map legend and the report read the same five values the cells were coloured by.
- **Test lessons.** A site at the centre of a gradient makes the distance to it collinear with the gradient; the run test's site stands 3 km north and the distance's interval is asserted to hold zero. The habitat tests skip where `hsa` does not import and run in the `analysis-image` job.

**The first run on dev (2026-10-08, Tim, project BPC).** One spotted hyena (SP100429), the last 7 days, NDVI and elevation, the defaults. It completed in 6 min 52 s: the NDVI batch job on openEO took 4 min 38 s and the DEM job 1 min 50 s, both fetched for real through the Copernicus account and cached (2.3 MB and 1.8 MB at 10 m over the 9 km by 6 km grid, 559,328 cells, EPSG:32734); the fit, the surface and the document took the remaining 14 s. The worker's memory stayed under 300 MB. The animal had 41 fixes in the week (96 percent missing by its expected interval), which thinning to twelve hours cut to 6 used and 60 available points; the coefficients came out with wide intervals (NDVI 0.16 ± 0.44, elevation −1.23 ± 0.73 on the standardised scale), the validation was skipped for one animal, and the 601 surface cells and the domain drew on the map with the legend by rank. Three defects came out of it, fixed the same day: the elevation layer's source read "Sentinel-2" (the provider now names each raster layer's source), the layers table showed the grid's 10 m for the 30 m DEM (it shows the layer's own resolution), and nothing warned that only 6 fixes carried the fit (`few_used`, under `FEW_FIXES`); the cards also printed a count with decimals. A rerun over the same area and period reads both rasters from the cache and should take seconds.

**The second day's runs on dev (2026-10-08, Okonjima).** Tim's run on one animal over a week failed in the worker on fewer than twenty fixes (the estimate now refuses that before the run is queued, through `min_fixes` on the module). His run on the same animal over 30 days completed. The example run that followed, four animals over 30 days, failed inside hrHSA's validation with "Found array with 0 sample(s)": the raster cache keyed the area by the bounding box rounded to the whole degree, so the four animals' larger grid was served the single animal's smaller rasters, most cells had no value, and one fold had no rows. The key now rounds to a hundred-thousandth of a degree, the module warns when a layer has a value in fewer than nine in ten of the grid's cells (`layer_sparse`), and the slope is offered whenever the provider answers the elevation (the first catalogue left it out).

**The example run, second attempt.** Four animals over 30 days on the corrected cache: the grid came out at about 340 km² at 10 m (3.4 million cells, under the 4 million bound), and the NDVI batch job on openEO was still running after the worker's 900 s: the job's input grows with the area and the number of Sentinel-2 scenes in the period (the one-animal week on BPC was 4.5 megapixels of input and 215 s on the Copernicus side). Three changes: the habitat module carries a timeout of its own, an hour, which the runner takes over the shared 900 s; the provider's rasters of a run are fetched at the same time (`cached_rasters`), so the DEM no longer waits for the NDVI; the module logs the grid and the fetch time. The guide says a first run over a large area and a long period waits, and that a rerun reads the cache. Not done: asking openEO for the NDVI at the grid's resolution before the cloud mask and the mean (the process resamples after them), which may or may not cut the job's input; and a run that survives a worker restart while a job runs (section 9).

**The example run, third attempt (four subjects, 14 days).** Completed in 14 min 27 s, 12.8 of them the NDVI job over a 2.6 million cell grid; the Boyce curves, the four domains and 708 surface cells rendered. Two findings. openEO answered 429 when the DEM job was started in the same second as the NDVI job, so the run went on with NDVI alone and two `layer_failed` warnings: a job request refused with 429 is now retried with a doubling wait (`Retry-After` when they say), and a job that would not start is deleted on their side.
